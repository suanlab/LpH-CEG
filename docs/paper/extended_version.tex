\section{The $\ell_p$ Composition Inequality}

\paragraph{Setup.} A hyperpath $P=(\joinstate_0{=}\emptyset,\block_1,
\joinstate_1,\dots,\block_m,\joinstate_m)$ adds a block of relations
$\block_j$ at step $j$, $\joinstate_j=\joinstate_{j-1}\cup\block_j$, and
$\joinstate_m$ is the set of all relations. Write $A_j=\attr{\block_j}$,
$V_j=\attr{\joinstate_j}$ ($V_0=\emptyset$, $V_m=V$), the interface
$\interface_j=A_j\cap V_{j-1}$ and $E_j=A_j\setminus\interface_j$. Then
\begin{itemize}
\item[(F1)] $E_j=V_j\setminus V_{j-1}$, so $\{E_j\}_{j}$ partitions $V$;
\item[(F2)] $\interface_j\subseteq V_{j-1}$.
\end{itemize}
Weights $w_{j,p}\ge0$ ($p\in\mathcal{P}\subseteq[1,\infty]$) satisfy the
attribute-wise cover constraint of the paper: for every attribute $a\in V$,
bound at step $j(a)$ (i.e.\ $a\in E_{j(a)}$),
\begin{equation}\tag{C}
w_{j(a)}+\sum_{j':\,a\in\interface_{j'}}s_{j'}\;\ge\;1,
\qquad w_j:=\sum_p w_{j,p},\quad s_j:=\sum_p w_{j,p}/p\ \ (1/\infty=0).
\end{equation}
A \emph{polymatroid} is $h:2^V\to\mathbb{R}$ with $h(\emptyset)=0$,
monotone and submodular; $h(X\mid Y):=h(X\cup Y)-h(Y)$.

\begin{lemma}[Conditioning]\label{lem:cond}
For a polymatroid $h$ and $Y\subseteq Z$: $h(X\mid Y)\ge h(X\mid Z)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Submodularity on $X\cup Y$ and $Z$ gives $h(X\cup Y)+h(Z)\ge
h(X\cup Y\cup Z)+h((X\cup Y)\cap Z)$, and $h((X\cup Y)\cap Z)\ge h(Y)$ by
monotonicity since $Y\subseteq(X\cup Y)\cap Z$. Rearrange.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fractional subadditivity]\label{lem:frac}
Let $f$ be monotone submodular on subsets of a finite set with
$f(\emptyset)=0$, and let $\lambda_i\ge0$ satisfy
$\sum_{i:\,a\in T_i}\lambda_i\ge1$ for every $a\in S$. Then
$\sum_i\lambda_if(T_i)\ge f(S)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order $S=(a_1,\dots,a_k)$ and let $S_{<t}=\{a_1,\dots,a_{t-1}\}$. By
monotonicity, the chain rule and Lemma~\ref{lem:cond},
$f(T_i)\ge f(T_i\cap S)=\sum_{a_t\in T_i}f(a_t\mid T_i\cap S_{<t})
\ge\sum_{a_t\in T_i}f(a_t\mid S_{<t})$. Weighting by $\lambda_i$ and
exchanging sums, $\sum_i\lambda_if(T_i)\ge\sum_t f(a_t\mid S_{<t})
\sum_{i:\,a_t\in T_i}\lambda_i\ge\sum_t f(a_t\mid S_{<t})=f(S)$, using
$f(a_t\mid S_{<t})\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The obligation]\label{lem:obl}
For every polymatroid $h$ and every $w$ satisfying (C),
\[
\sum_j\Bigl[s_j\,h(\interface_j)+w_j\,h(E_j\mid\interface_j)\Bigr]\;\ge\;h(V).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $g_j=h(E_j\mid V_{j-1})$, $\delta_j=\max(0,1-w_j)\in[0,1]$ and
$f_j(S)=h(S\mid V_{j-1})$ for $S\subseteq E_j$; $f_j$ is monotone,
submodular and $f_j(\emptyset)=0$.

(i) For each $j'$, the chain rule along the partition (F1) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:cond} give
$h(\interface_{j'})=\sum_j h(\interface_{j'}\cap E_j\mid
\interface_{j'}\cap V_{j-1})\ge\sum_j f_j(\interface_{j'}\cap E_j)$.
Hence $\sum_{j'}s_{j'}h(\interface_{j'})\ge\sum_j\sum_{j'}s_{j'}
f_j(\interface_{j'}\cap E_j)$.

(ii) Fix $j$. If $\delta_j>0$ then by (C) every $a\in E_j$ has
$\sum_{j':a\in\interface_{j'}}s_{j'}\ge1-w_j=\delta_j$, so the sets
$\interface_{j'}\cap E_j$ with weights $s_{j'}/\delta_j$ fractionally
cover $E_j$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:frac} gives
$\sum_{j'}s_{j'}f_j(\interface_{j'}\cap E_j)\ge\delta_j\,g_j$. If
$\delta_j=0$ the same inequality holds because the left side is
non-negative.

(iii) By (F2) and Lemma~\ref{lem:cond}, $h(E_j\mid\interface_j)\ge g_j\ge0$,
and $w_j\ge1-\delta_j$, so $w_jh(E_j\mid\interface_j)\ge(1-\delta_j)g_j$.

Adding (i)--(iii): the left side is at least
$\sum_j[\delta_j+(1-\delta_j)]g_j=\sum_jh(E_j\mid V_{j-1})=h(V)$ by the
chain rule and (F1).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\ell_p$ Composition Inequality]
Let $D$ be a database, $\query(D)\ne\emptyset$, and let
$\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}$ be the $\ell_p$-norm of the degree
sequence of $E_j$-values per $\interface_j$-value in a relation
$R_j\supseteq\pi_{A_j}\query(D)$---for instance the block join
$\bowtie_{R\in\block_j}R$ computed exactly on $D$. If $w$ satisfies (C), then
\[
|\query(D)|\;\le\;\prod_{j=1}^m\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}
\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}^{\,w_{j,p}}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $h$ be the entropy of the uniform distribution on $\query(D)$; it is a
polymatroid and $h(V)=\log_2|\query(D)|$. The marginal on $A_j$ is
supported on $\pi_{A_j}\query(D)\subseteq R_j$, so the $\ell_p$ degree
inequality of Abo Khamis et al.\ (PODS 2024) gives, for $p\in[1,\infty]$,
$\frac1p h(\interface_j)+h(E_j\mid\interface_j)\le
\log_2\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}$. Multiply by $w_{j,p}\ge0$, sum, and
apply Lemma~\ref{lem:obl}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope.} The proof uses only monotonicity and submodularity, so
it holds over the whole polymatroid cone and for every hyperpath, block
size and $p\ge1$. It requires (C) exactly: an LP solver may return weights
violating (C) by about $10^{-7}$; scaling $w$ by
$1/(1-\varepsilon)$, for a coverage shortfall $\varepsilon=1-\min_a(\text{coverage of }a)$,
restores (C) and raises a bound $B$ by the factor $B^{\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)}$, which grows
with $B$. At the solver's feasibility tolerance ($\varepsilon\le10^{-7}$) and the largest
cover bound we report ($B<2^{100}$) the factor is below $1+10^{-5}$; the released
code applies it; the bounds reported in the paper were computed before it did
and sit below their rescaled values by at most that factor. It requires exact statistics of the
database being bounded (or deterministic upper bounds on them, since the
bound is monotone in each $\mu$); a sampled or sketched statistic, such as
the Hutchinson estimate of the common-neighbour $\ell_2$ in the paper, is
outside the guarantee. When some block join is empty the query is empty
and the inequality holds trivially (the released LP gives such a factor
weight zero, which only loosens the bound).

\begin{proposition}[Necessity]
If nonnegative weights $(w_{j,p})$ make the product above an upper bound on
$|\query(D)|$ for every database $D$ whose atoms' relations are
independent (no two atoms are copies of one relation), they satisfy (C).
Hence on a fixed hyperpath the cover LP is optimal over all weightings of
its statistics that are valid on every such database.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $a\in V$ and $N\ge2$, and let $D_N$ give $a$ the domain $[N]$ and every
other attribute a single value, each relation holding all tuples over
these domains. Then $|\query(D_N)|=N$, and the block join of $\block_j$ has
$N$ tuples if $a\in A_j$ and one otherwise, so
$\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}$ is $N^{1/p}$ if $a\in\interface_j$, $N$
if $a\in E_j$, and $1$ if $a\notin A_j$. The bound is $N^{c_a}$ with $c_a$
the left side of (C) at $a$, and $N^{c_a}\ge N$ forces $c_a\ge1$. (The
entropy of $D_N$'s join is the modular polymatroid $h(S)=[a\in S]\log_2N$,
so the proposition also holds over the polymatroid cone.)
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Self-joins.} The construction sets each atom's relation
independently, which a self-join forbids: in $R(a,b)\bowtie R(b,c)\bowtie
R(c,a)$ the three atoms share $R$, $D_N$ is not a database of that query,
and indeed weight $3$ on the first step's $|R|$ and $0$ elsewhere gives the
valid bound $|R|^3$ although $c$ is uncovered. Sufficiency (the
Composition Inequality) is unaffected, since every self-join database is
also a database of the independent query; necessity, and with it the
reading that reweighting a fixed hyperpath's statistics is exhausted,
holds for independent atoms only.

\paragraph{Why the elementary argument does not suffice.} If $w_j\ge1$ for
every step, Lemma~\ref{lem:obl} follows from (iii) alone. The LP's optima
on cyclic queries have $w_j<1$ (e.g.\ $1/3$ on the closing step of a
cycle); the deficit $\delta_j$ is paid for by later interfaces, which is
exactly step (ii). The cyclic improvement and the need for
Lemma~\ref{lem:frac} have the same source.

\paragraph{Independent checks.} An LP over the polymatroid cone decides
Lemma~\ref{lem:obl} at fixed weights; enumerating every vertex of the
feasible weight region decides it for all weights. Over 6{,}197 hyperpath
structures on eleven shapes with $|V|\le6$ and block sizes $1$--$3$ the
minimum slack is $0$ to solver precision; an adversarial search over random abstract
hyperpath schemes with $|V|\le5$ found no violation; and over $207{,}000$ database--block-size checks the bound never fell below the exact count
(\texttt{scripts/shannon\_check\_wide.py},
\texttt{scripts/validity\_search.py}).

\section{The Cyclic Improvement theorem}

Order the cycle $C_k$ as $R_1(a_1,a_2),\dots,R_k(a_k,a_1)$ and extend in
this order with single-relation blocks. Let $N_j=|R_j|$, $M_j$ the maximum
degree and $\mu_p(R_j)$ the $\ell_p$-norm of the degree sequence of $R_j$
from $a_j$ to $a_{j+1}$.

\paragraph{Structure.} Step 1 has interface $\emptyset$ and extension
$\{a_1,a_2\}$: its degree sequence is the single value $N_1$. A middle step
$2\le j\le k-1$ has interface $\{a_j\}$, extension $\{a_{j+1}\}$ and
$\mu_\infty=M_j$. The closing step $k$ has interface $\{a_k,a_1\}$ and
empty extension; its conditional degrees are all $1$, so $\mu_\infty=1$
and $\mu_1=N_k$. With $p=\infty$ and unit weights this gives
$B_{\max}=N_1\prod_{j=2}^{k-1}M_j$.

\paragraph{A feasible point for every common $p\ge1$.} Put weight
$\frac{p}{p+1}$ with exponent $p$ on every middle step, $\frac{p}{p+1}$ on
step 1 and $\frac1{p+1}$ on the closing step with exponent $1$. The cover
constraints read: $a_1$: $\frac p{p+1}+\frac1{p+1}=1$; $a_2$:
$\frac p{p+1}+\frac{p/(p+1)}{p}=1$; $a_i$ for $3\le i\le k-1$:
$\frac p{p+1}+\frac1{p+1}=1$; $a_k$: $\frac p{p+1}+\frac1{p+1}=1$. All are
tight, so the LP optimum satisfies
\[
B_{\mathrm{LP}}\le N_1^{\frac p{p+1}}N_k^{\frac1{p+1}}
\prod_{j=2}^{k-1}\mu_p(R_j)^{\frac p{p+1}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{B_{\max}}{B_{\mathrm{LP}}}\ge\Bigl(\frac{N_1}{N_k}\Bigr)^{\frac1{p+1}}
\prod_{j=2}^{k-1}\frac{M_j}{\mu_p(R_j)^{p/(p+1)}}.
\]
\paragraph{The exponent must be common (or non-increasing).} The
constraint of the attribute between middle edges $j$ and $j{+}1$ is
$\frac{p_j}{p_j+1}+\frac1{p_{j+1}+1}\ge1$, i.e.\ $p_{j+1}\le p_j$. Choosing
each edge's best exponent independently is therefore not feasible in
general (e.g.\ $p_j=1$, $p_{j+1}=\infty$ covers that attribute only to
$\frac12$); the LP may still mix exponents within a step.

\paragraph{Growth.} At $p=2$ a middle factor $M_j/\mu_2(R_j)^{2/3}$ exceeds
$1$ exactly when $\mu_2(R_j)^2<M_j^3$: the sum of squared degrees is
below the cube of the maximum degree. A few high-degree values satisfy it;
many low-degree ones need not ($n$ values of uniform degree $d$ qualify
iff $n<d$, and $10^4$ values of degree $1$ plus one of degree $20$ do not).
When every middle factor is at least $c>1$ the ratio grows geometrically
in the number $k-2$ of middle edges. As $p\to\infty$ every factor tends to $1$, and LpH-CEG
returns $\min(B_{\max},B_{\mathrm{LP}})$, so the realized ratio is always
at least $1$. The boundary factor $(N_1/N_k)^{1/(p+1)}$ does not depend on
$k$; it is $1$ for a uniform cycle.

\paragraph{Acyclic queries.} On a star the cover constraints force unit
weight on every step and the LP returns the max-degree bound. On a path the
LP can use an interface at $p=1$ in place of an extension (on a 4-path it
returns weights $(1,0,1)$), so acyclicity alone does not force unit
weights; what redundant coverage on cycles adds is the slack exploited
above.

\section{A spectral reading of the common-neighbour statistic (post hoc)}
For an even $k$-cycle of binary relations the
count is a trace, $|\query|=\mathrm{Tr}(A_1\cdots A_k)$ for the incidence
matrices $A_i$ ($v_i\times v_{i+1}$), and the common-neighbour $\ell_2$
norm is the Frobenius (Schatten-$2$) norm of the block product
$B=A_iA_{i+1}$. Hölder's inequality for Schatten norms gives, over $m=k/2$
consecutive blocks, $|\query|\le\prod_j\|B_j\|_{S_m}$, and
$\|B\|_{S_m}^m\le\sigma_{\max}(B)^{m-2}\|B\|_F^2$ with $\sigma_{\max}$
bounded above by a Collatz--Wielandt certificate on $B^{\top}B$. Two
numbers per block, both from the pair join ($\sigma_{\max}$ lies outside the
polymatroid catalog), give a bound tighter than the polymatroid program on
$96/240$ long-suite queries (by a median $1.25\times$ there, though $1.11\times$
looser over all) and never below an exact count; with each block's top
$24$ singular values (Lanczos; uncertified, yet above every exact count)
it is tighter on $220/240$ and takes IMDb at $|V|{=}10$ from
$14.9\times$ to $3.2\times$ the truth. The next step on the statistics axis may thus be spectral order rather
than block size.

\section{Which statistics are new: a vocabulary table and a separation}

For a binary block $R(a,b)\bowtie S(b,c)$ the statistics the paper uses or
compares against are the following ($\deg_R(b)$ is the number of $a$ with
$(a,b)\in R$, and $N_R(a)$ the set of $b$ with $(a,b)\in R$).

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Statistic & Definition & Pair join? & Used by \\
\midrule
single-relation norms & $\|\deg_R(\cdot\mid a)\|_p$, $\|\deg_R(\cdot\mid b)\|_p$ & no & LpBound, all programs \\
pair-join size & $\sum_b\deg_R(b)\deg_S(b)$ & no & CorrBound ($\ell_1$ inner product) \\
shared-attribute products & $\sum_b\deg_R(b)^{p}\deg_S(b)^{q}$ & no & CorrBound's generalized \\
 & & & inner products (per its authors) \\
one-side interface norms & $\big\|\big(\sum_{b\in N_R(a)}\deg_S(b)\big)_a\big\|_p$ & no & this paper (join-free) \\
common-neighbour norms & $\big\|\big(|N_R(a)\cap N_S(c)|\big)_{a,c}\big\|_p$ & yes & this paper \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[Separation]\label{prop:separation}
The common-neighbour $\ell_2$ norm is not determined by the other rows of
the table: there are two databases that agree on every single-relation
degree sequence, on the pair-join size, on every shared-attribute product
$\sum_b\deg_R(b)^p\deg_S(b)^q$ ($p,q\ge0$) and on every one-side interface
norm, yet differ in the squared common-neighbour $\ell_2$ norm ($32$
against $24$), and so in the number of $4$-cycles they close.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $R$ with the $4\times4$ incidence matrix $M$ and $S$ with $M^{\top}$,
for $M=A$, two disjoint copies of $K_{2,2}$, and $M=B$, the bipartite
$8$-cycle (row $i$ adjacent to columns $i$ and $i{+}1 \bmod 4$). Both are
$2$-regular, so every degree in $R$ and $S$ is $2$, every shared-attribute
product is $4\cdot2^{p+q}$, the pair-join size is $16$, and every one-side
interface degree is $4$. The common-neighbour matrix is $MM^{\top}$: for
$A$ it has eight entries equal to $2$ (squared norm $32$); for $B$, four
diagonal entries $2$ and eight entries $1$ (squared norm $24$); the largest
common-neighbour count is $2$ in both. For the $4$-cycle
$R(a,b)\bowtie S(b,c)\bowtie R'(c,d)\bowtie S'(d,a)$ with $R'=R$, $S'=S$
as separate atoms, the count is $\mathrm{tr}((MM^{\top})^2)=\|MM^{\top}\|_F^2$,
again $32$ against $24$.
\end{proof}

The separation is limited to the statistics listed: it does not claim that
no multi-relation term of CorrBound, or any other statistic, determines the
common-neighbour norm, and it says nothing about the size of the gain on
real data (the paper's ablations measure that).

\section{Which statistic needs the join: three-norm details, sketch and selection}
Of the eight interfaces of a block of binary relations $R(a,b),S(b,c)$,
seven (five non-trivial) come from a scan of one relation with degree lookups in the other
(the degree of $a$ is $\sum_{b\in N_R(a)}\deg_S(b)$, that of $b$ is
$\deg_R(b)\deg_S(b)$); only $\{a,c\}$, the common-neighbour count
$|N_R(a)\cap N_S(c)|$, needs the pair join; its $\ell_2$ is determined neither by the other interfaces nor by shared-attribute inner products (Section on the separating example), while its $\ell_1$ is the join size. We compute the seven without the join (identical to
the materialized statistics on $120/120$ Hetionet queries; $0.49$\,s
against $4.6$\,s per block). With three norms and the flow LP they stop a
median $2.3\times$ (max $18.7\times$) above the full $bs{=}2$ bound on the
Hetionet long suite ($1.5$--$2.9\times$ on the families, $1.9$--$57\times$
on the sweep), and the common-neighbour $\ell_2$, $\|A_R^{\top}A_S\|_F$,
closes the gap on $12/12$ family--seed pairs, $10/10$ sweep points,
$85/120$ Hetionet and $116/120$ IMDb queries (Hetionet median
$1.00\times$, max $1.67\times$); its $\ell_\infty$ does not ($1.70\times$),
and added to $bs{=}1$ alone it reaches $1.03\times$ ($53/120$ exact), so
the attribution is order-dependent. In the headline setting (eleven norms,
polymatroid) the join-free seven carry a median $88\%$ and $86\%$ of the
log-gain on Hetionet and IMDb, the $\ell_2$ leaves $1.47\times$ and
$1.39\times$ (full on $1/120$ each), and the other common-neighbour norms (with the join-free $\ell_1$) close the rest. A one-pass triangle-inequality bound on the $\ell_2$
recovers nothing on Hetionet ($2.2\times$); a $64$-probe Hutchinson sketch
lands within $0.91$--$1.78\times$ of the full bound in $0.32$\,s per block
but is an estimate, not a bound. Post hoc, it picks which joins to pay
for: joining the three of a $|V|{=}6$ query's six blocks whose sketched
norm lowers the bound most recovers the full $bs{\le}2$ bound on the median
of $34$ Hetionet queries (top two on $32$ IMDb ones), against half that
gain for a random three; timed end to end, this plan takes a median
$0.67\times$ the time of joining every block on Hetionet ($33/40$ cheaper)
but $1.39\times$ on IMDb, where join-free statistics, sketches and ranking
cost more than the three joins they skip.
These times are against materialized pair joins. The sparse-product path
of the paper's Table~V computes every common-neighbour norm without join
tuples, for $8.2\times$ and $8.3\times$ LpBound's collection, so choosing
which blocks to join matters only where the joins are materialized.


\section{Which block size to pay for: cost-study details}
At $bs{=}1$, collecting degree sequences on the four cost-study families
costs $22$--$27$\,ms against ${\sim}1.9$\,ms to solve; $bs{\ge}2$
multiplies that term by a median $45\times$ there ($24\times$ more
entries) and $61\times$ ($8$--$558\times$) on the Hetionet suite, and a
cataloged $bs{=}2$ estimate costs $10$\,ms (the paper's long-suite table).
Use $bs{=}1$ when the bound is re-solved many times per statistics build
and $bs{\ge}2$ when the pair joins can be materialized ahead of the
workload.


\section{IMDb at five times the films, and the YAGO count gap}
\emph{IMDb at $1{,}000$ films.} At five times the films of the paper's slice ($1{,}000$; the $40$ queries at
$|V|{=}6$, bounds only, three norms; exploratory) every tightness ratio
grows: the inference gap from $3.8$ to $4.8\times$, block statistics from
$4.0$ to $7.0\times$ under the cover LP and from $17$ to $48\times$ under
the flow LP, $40/40$ below entropic, the program gap from $4.2$ to
$6.8\times$. Collecting the statistics grows faster than the data:
$4.2\times$ the rows cost $48$\,s per query at the median (from
$4.4$\,s), up to $25$\,min and $16$\,GiB, measured with $10$ workers
sharing the machine.

\emph{YAGO counts.} Our exact counts differ from G-CARE's on $49$ of the $192$ YAGO queries we evaluate. A
second implementation of the same semantics that reads G-CARE's raw files
without DuckDB reproduces our count on all $49$ of them, and $45$ of them lie in
G-CARE's Petal families ($45$ of $100$ Petal queries, against $4$ of $92$
others), so the gap is in semantics or preprocessing we could not reconstruct, not settled either way.

\section{Degree sequences from catalog histograms}
Degree sequences can come from catalog histograms at \texttt{ANALYZE} time: over buckets $(d_i, c_i)$ (degree $d_i$ held by $c_i$ values), $\|\mathbf{d}\|_p = (\sum_i c_i d_i^p)^{1/p}$. We have not evaluated this.

\section{CorrBound's cyclic workload}
CorrBound's public artifact (\url{https://github.com/christophmayerm/CorrBound}, MIT) ships the
workload of its cyclic experiment: $400$ labelled subgraph-matching queries
with $8$ vertices ($200$ dense, $200$ sparse; $11$--$28$ edges) on DBLP
($317$K vertices, $2.1$M directed edge rows, symmetric), its estimates for
CorrBound-e (exact degree vectors) and LpBound (flow bound), and true
cardinalities. Each edge atom becomes a binary relation over its two query
vertices holding the edges whose endpoints carry the query's labels; the
vertex atoms are key lookups with a label predicate, and CorrBound's own LP
drops them too. CorrBound-e's and the artifact LpBound's estimates are
taken from the artifact's result files, not recomputed.

\emph{Same norms, same baseline.} CorrBound's LpBound statistics use
$p\in\{1,\dots,30,\infty\}$, and so do we. Our single-relation polymatroid
bound reproduces the artifact's LpBound estimate on $400/400$ queries
(ratio $1.000000$ at both extremes; the artifact computes it with the
flow LP and we with the polymatroid program, which coincide for
single-relation degree constraints), so both sides start from the same
statistics and the same baseline.

\emph{Results.} Block statistics ($bs{\le}2$, every interface) under the
polymatroid program sit a median $1.99\times$ above the exact count (P95
$3.9\times$), against $6.1\times$ (P95 $20\times$) for CorrBound-e and
$64.6\times$ (P95 $190\times$) for LpBound. They are tighter than CorrBound-e
on $382/400$ queries ($191$ of each kind), a median $3.0\times$, and at most
$1.51\times$ looser on the other $18$. No bound of any method falls below the
exact count.

\emph{The counts.} The artifact's true cardinalities sit below the count its
own SQL returns on its own data on all $400$ queries (a median $0.6\%$, at
most $39\%$); we checked three queries by running the artifact's SQL
verbatim, and requiring distinct images for same-label vertices does not
reproduce them. We therefore check validity against a recount with DuckDB
over the same relations; against the artifact's counts no bound falls below
either.

\emph{What each side has.} CorrBound-e adds, for every query vertex with $k$
incident edges, the $k$-way generalized inner products of their degree
vectors ($p=1,2,3$) and intra-relation correlation norms; our $bs{\le}2$
catalog has neither. It has the interface-conditioned norms of every pair
block, which CorrBound's vocabulary lacks for the one-side and
common-neighbour interfaces (Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}).
CorrBound's statistics are computed once per dataset; ours cost a median
$25$\,s per query in our Python prototype (all pair blocks, $31$ norms,
materialized joins), and the solve $0.38$\,s.

\emph{Re-running CorrBound-e.} We ran the artifact's own LP builder
(\texttt{corrbound.cyclic.LPBuilder}, OR-Tools GLOP; its flow-LP extension,
unused by CorrBound-e, stubbed) on statistics we computed in its formats
from its DBLP files (\texttt{scripts/corrbound\_sm\_artifact\_lp.py}). Run
unchanged it reproduces the published estimate on $400/400$ queries
(ratio $1.000000$ at both extremes). Its inter-relation inequality is
written $p\sum_i h(xy_i) - (k-1)h(x) \le \log_2\sum_v\prod_i d_i(v)^p$ and
its intra-relation one $(p+q+1)h(AB) - h(A) - h(B) \le \log_2\sum_{(s,t)}
d(s)^p d(t)^q$, for every $p\in\{1,2,3\}$ and every $p,q\le 10$ with $pq\le 12$ respectively. The paper's Eq.~(13) and (14),
$h(x)+p\sum_i h(y_i\mid x)$ and $h(AB)+p\,h(B\mid A)+q\,h(A\mid B)$ on the
left, give $-(pk-1)h(x)$ and $-p\,h(A)-q\,h(B)$; the two agree at
$p{=}q{=}1$. For larger $p$ the coded forms are not implied by the Lemma:
on a complete bipartite relation $K_{m,m}$ the uniform distribution makes
the coded intra-relation left side $8\log_2 m$ at $p{=}q{=}2$, against a
right side of $6\log_2 m$. With Eq.~(13)/(14) and nothing else changed,
the builder's estimates are a median $2.4\times$ ($1.5$--$6.1\times$)
higher, $15.2\times$ above the exact count; neither version falls below an
exact count on these queries. The paper's comparison uses the published
estimates, which is the conservative comparison for block statistics; against the Eq.~(13)/(14) version,
block statistics are a median $7.3\times$ tighter.

\emph{Combined catalog.} Adding CorrBound-e's inter- and intra-relation
inequalities (Eq.~(13)/(14)) to our polymatroid program over the
$bs{\le}2$ block catalog (\texttt{scripts/corrbound\_sm\_combined.py}; the
program gets \texttt{polymatroid\_bound}'s \texttt{extra} constraints)
tightens $28$ of the $400$ queries, by at most $3.1\%$ (median over the
exact count $1.985\times$ against $1.987\times$). On this workload the
block statistics carry nearly all of what CorrBound-e's statistics add. In the same
program, CorrBound-e's statistics alone give $15.1\times$ over the exact
count, against $15.2\times$ in the artifact's LP, whose Shannon cone omits
the $h(x)+h(y)\ge h(xy)$ inequalities.

\section{Condensed in the main text}
Three passages are shortened in the paper. \emph{Pair-join sizes on IMDb} (the paper's IMDb paragraph keeps the self-join explanation): where half the
pairs are self-joins, sizes add almost nothing over LpBound, while the
interface-conditioned statistics add $17$--$171\times$. \emph{Collection cost:} the
$61\times$ overhead of blocks, growing faster than the data, calls for
cataloging inside the system rather than per-query collection.
\emph{Join evaluation:} Yannakakis+ makes Yannakakis-style evaluation of

residuals could supply if they qualify, though in our $66$-query study they
do not beat max-degree ordering in general.

\section{Intermediate norms on an acyclic path}
Theorem~4 (cyclic improvement) is a sufficient condition. Intermediate norms
can also tighten the cover bound on a path. Take
$Q = R(a,b)\bowtie S(b,c)\bowtie T(c,d)$ with $R=\{(a_0,b_0)\}$,
$S=\{(b_0,c_i) : 0\le i<10\}$ and
$T=\{(c_0,d_j) : 0\le j<100\}\cup\{(c_i,d_0) : 1\le i\le 900\}$, so
$|R|,|S|,|T| = 1, 10, 1{,}000$ and $|Q| = 109$. On the hyperpath
$R,S,T$ with $bs{=}1$, the cover LP gives $1{,}000$ with
$\mathcal{P}=\{1,\infty\}$ and $\sqrt{10}\cdot\sqrt{10{,}900}\approx 330.15$
with $\mathcal{P}=\{1,2,\infty\}$: weights $(1,\tfrac12,1)$ with exponents
$(1,1,2)$ cover the attributes $(a,b,c,d)$ at $(1,1.5,1,1)$, and the
$\ell_2$ of $T$'s $c$-degrees ($\sqrt{100^2+900}$) replaces part of $S$'s
extension. The cardinalities are the same on both sides; only $\ell_2$ is
added. A second instance (with $|S|=22$, $|T|=100$) goes from $100$ to
$\sqrt{20\cdot 190}\approx 61.64$. Both values are what the released
estimator returns, and LpBound's entropic bound on the same statistics
equals them. Our experiments do not measure how often this happens on
acyclic workloads, and the stars we ran gain nothing (each leaf is covered
by one step only).

\section{Cost measurement details}
The script \texttt{cost\_accuracy\_indep.py} behind the paper's
Table~V runs every (query, catalog) pair as its own task in a fresh
process ($8$ workers, shuffled order, one run), times collection (data loading excluded), the

the catalogs of a query in one process, in a fixed order, with the full

\section{Additional $bs=1$ experiments: JOB, TPC-H-like, data size and skew}

On the JOB-medium subset (CI: 3K, MK: 2K, MC: 1K rows) the two co-actor
4-cycles improve $1.94\times$ (with keywords) and $2.80\times$ (with
companies) at $bs=1$ with weights $w{=}0.5$, and a star query by
exactly $1.00\times$; the IMDb suite of the paper is the
systematic version. On a small TPC-H-like synthetic schema (SF${=}0.01$; not \texttt{dbgen} data) every cyclic query we built yields $1.00\times$; LpH-CEG needs \emph{cyclic structure} together with \emph{degree skew} in a middle relation. On synthetic cycle-4 (10 seeds) the improvement grows
with data size, from $5.9{\pm}0.7\times$ at $100$ vertices to
$33.9{\pm}1.5\times$ at $2$K (relative std ${<}12\%$; a single-run sweep
reaches $57\times$ at $5$K), and peaks at moderate skew: $2.3{\pm}0.5\times$
at $\alpha{=}0.5$, $16.1{\pm}0.8\times$ at $\alpha{=}2.0$,
$6.6{\pm}0.4\times$ at $\alpha{=}3.0$. At low skew all $\ell_p$-norms
nearly coincide, at extreme skew $\ell_\infty$ is already near-tight, and
in between $\ell_2$ captures the heavy tail.

Every number in this section is produced by the scripts in the repository
and checked by its claim audit.

\section{Multi-way ordering by per-step residuals}
The per-step residuals can order a
worst-case-optimal join greedily. As implemented, the residual of a variable
prefix is the LP re-solved on the query with the prefix attributes projected
out, which bounds the residual for every value of the prefix (and could be
computed for LpBound in the same way): GenericJoin iterations drop $2.3\times$
on the Hetionet cycle-4 in our simulator, but over $66$ synthetic queries
the bound-guided order is $1.3\%$ worse at the median ($20$ wins, $43$
losses; Wilcoxon $p{=}0.003$, indicative, as queries share shapes and
seeds), so it is a targeted heuristic.

\section{Complexity}
Collecting $\muLp{p}{\block}{\interface}$ for a single relation is one
scan, $O(N)$; for a two-relation block it materializes the pair join,
except for the interfaces computable from a scan of one relation with degree lookups in the other
(all but the common-neighbour interface of a binary block; paragraph
``Which statistic needs the join'' of the paper). The cover LP has $m|\mathcal P|$ variables and $|V|$
constraints; it solves in milliseconds for the queries in the paper. The
flow LP over a $bs\le2$ catalog is polynomial in the number of constraints
and solves in $0.03$--$4.4$\,s at $k=3$--$16$ over $4k$ orders.
