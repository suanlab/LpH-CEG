\begin{abstract}
Pessimistic cardinality bounds are usually improved by strengthening
the \emph{program} combining degree statistics, from fractional edge covers to LpBound's Shannon-cone LP. We ask where the headroom lies for cyclic joins, using LpH-CEG, a fractional block-cover LP over $\ell_p$-norm degree
sequences of multi-way blocks, and find it in the statistics. On single-relation statistics little is left: on a fixed hyperpath the cover LP is provably optimal among weightings valid on every self-join-free database, and LpBound's flow LP
reaches the polymatroid optimum. Block statistics, degree sequences of
two-relation joins conditioned on their interfaces, enter that program unchanged. With eleven norms on both sides they tighten LpBound by a median
$236$--$10{,}200\times$ on $120$ rule-selected Hetionet cycles ($|V|{=}6$--$10$) and $9.7$--$87\times$ on $120$ IMDb cycles, below LpBound on all $349$
Hetionet and IMDb queries, and $15$--$24\times$ on G-CARE ($241$ AIDS, $192$ of $366$ YAGO
queries). On CorrBound's own $400$ subgraph-matching queries they are a median $3.0\times$ tighter than its published estimates. A median $48\%$ (Hetionet) and $79\%$ (IMDb) of the log-gain lies outside a pairwise inner-product proxy for CorrBound's statistics, and all statistics but one avoid the join: with one sparse product per block the full catalog costs about $8\times$ LpBound's collection, against $25$--$54\times$ with materialized joins. We prove validity via an $\ell_p$ Composition Inequality, a Shannon-flow inequality; an $\ell_p$ flow LP gives a polynomial-time candidate, checked per query. Limits:
statistics come from data slices, guaranteed polymatroid bounds stop at $|V|{=}12$,
and bound-guided join orders lose to DuckDB on IMDb.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
cardinality estimation, cyclic queries, $\ell_p$-norms, block statistics, polymatroid bound, pessimistic estimation
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cardinality estimates drive query optimization; their errors compound multiplicatively across joins, and cyclic queries are the hardest case, since cycle-closing edges break the independence assumptions of most estimators.

Pessimistic estimation replaces estimates by guaranteed upper
bounds~\cite{abokhamis2025pessimistic}.
The AGM bound~\cite{atserias2008agm} uses fractional edge covers;
PANDA~\cite{abokhamis2017panda} and the degree-based MOLP
bound~\cite{joglekar2016molp} add max-degree ($\ell_\infty$)
constraints; and most recently,
\textbf{LpBound}~\cite{zhang2025lpbound} generalizes to all
$\ell_p$-norms of degree sequences, achieving orders-of-magnitude
improvements on skewed data via an LP over entropy inequalities.
Independently, statistics over small multi-relation joins~\cite{chen2022ceg,salihoglu2026ceg} and multi-way \emph{block extensions}~\cite{hceg2027} capture joint constraints across relations.

However, the two lines have not met: \emph{to our knowledge, no published pessimistic bound uses interface-conditioned $\ell_p$ norms of multi-relation (block) degree sequences} (CEG$_M$ uses their $\ell_\infty$ case; CorrBound's generalized inner products overlap the shared-interface case; Section~\ref{sec:related}).
LpBound's program exploits the redundant coverage of cyclic queries, but
over single-relation statistics only; block extension methods capture
joint constraints, but through max degrees only.

\paragraph{Our contribution}
We separate what a better \emph{program} and what richer
\emph{statistics} contribute to cyclic join bounds, and find the
headroom on the statistics side. The instrument is LpH-CEG, a
\emph{fractional block cover LP} over $\ell_p$-norm degree sequences of
multi-way blocks: on cyclic queries an attribute is covered by several
steps, and the slack admits fractional weights $w_j<1$ that, with
intermediate norms ($\ell_2$), tighten the max-degree bound ($w{=}1$,
$p{=}\infty$). On single-relation statistics it cannot beat the entropic
optimum (Figure~\ref{fig:positioning}); what it exposes is that
\emph{block statistics} move the bound far below that optimum, and that
on them a stronger program pays off again. The estimator this leads to
is block statistics with polymatroid inference.

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The estimator and its tightness
(Sections~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference},~\ref{sec:scalability}).}
With eleven norms on both sides, block statistics
$\mu_p(\block\mid\interface)$, $|\block|\ge2$, in the polymatroid program
are a median $236$--$10{,}200\times$ below LpBound on $120$ rule-selected
Hetionet cycles ($|V|{=}6$--$10$), $9.7$--$87\times$ on $120$ IMDb cycles
and $15$--$24\times$ on G-CARE ($433$ AIDS and YAGO queries run), and a
median $3.0\times$ below CorrBound-e's published estimates on CorrBound's
own $400$ subgraph-matching queries. On these statistics the program
matters again: the polymatroid optimum beats the cover LP by a median
$5.0$--$11.6\times$, against $2.7$--$6.8\times$ on single-relation
statistics.

\item \textbf{Which statistics matter, and their cost
(Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).}
Pair sizes and pairwise inner products (a proxy for CorrBound's statistics)
gain $42\times$ (Hetionet) and $2.0\times$ (IMDb); the one-side and
common-neighbour statistics add $34\times$ and $14\times$ on $120/120$
queries. All interfaces but one come from a scan of one relation with
degree lookups in the other; the common-neighbour sequence needs one
sparse product per block, and its $\ell_2$ is determined by none of the
others (extended version). The full catalog thus costs about $8\times$
LpBound's collection, against $25$--$54\times$ with the pair joins
materialized.

\item \textbf{On single-relation statistics the inference axis is closed
(Sections~\ref{sec:theory},~\ref{sec:vs-lpbound}).}
On a fixed hyperpath the cover LP is optimal among weightings valid on
every self-join-free database (Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity}), yet sits
$2.7$--$6.8\times$ above the entropic optimum, which LpBound's flow LP
reaches in polynomial time. An $\ell_p$ Composition Inequality makes the
cover bound valid for every hyperpath, block size and $p$ ($207$K
database--block-size checks and an LP certificate over $6{,}197$
hyperpath structures agree); our $\ell_p$ flow LP is a polynomial-time
candidate for the polymatroid optimum (equal on $633$ of $862$ queries),
checked per query.

\item \textbf{Limits (Sections~\ref{sec:experiments},~\ref{sec:wcoj}).}
Guiding join order, the bound trims the catastrophic tail on $24$ small
synthetic cyclic queries (P95 slowdown $1.2\times$ vs.\ $5.8\times$, from
the denser shapes) rather than the average; on IMDb block statistics
pick the exact-count order on $24/40$ queries, yet those left-deep plans
lose to DuckDB's own; a per-step residual ordering heuristic does not
generalize (median $-1.3\%$ over $66$ queries); and a predicate-free
catalog helps only under mild predicates.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Query Hypergraphs}

A conjunctive query $\query$ over relations $\rel_1, \ldots, \rel_m$
with attributes $V = \{a_1, \ldots, a_n\}$ is represented as a
hypergraph $H = (V, \mathcal{E})$ where each hyperedge $e_i \in
\mathcal{E}$ corresponds to the attribute set of relation $\rel_i$.
The full natural join is $\query = \bowtie_{i=1}^{m} \rel_i$, under set semantics (relations are sets of tuples).

\subsection{CEG and MOLP}

Cardinality Estimation Graphs~\cite{chen2022ceg,salihoglu2026ceg} model
both optimistic and pessimistic estimators as path-finding in a directed
graph.
The pessimistic CEG$_M$ uses max-degree edge weights: for relation
$\rel$ with interface $\interface$,
$\muFactor{\rel}{\interface} = \max_{a_\interface}
|\ext{\rel}{a_\interface}|$.
The MOLP bound~\cite{joglekar2016molp} equals the minimum-weight path in CEG$_M$~\cite{chen2022ceg}.

\subsection{Block Extensions and Hyperpaths}

A \emph{block} $\block \subseteq \mathcal{E}$ is a connected subset
of relations.
The \emph{block extension factor}~\cite{hceg2027} is
$\muFactor{\block}{\interface} = \max_{a_\interface}
|\ext{\block}{a_\interface}|$,
where $\interface = \attr{\joinstate} \cap \attr{\block}$ is the
interface with the current state.
A \emph{hyperpath} $P = (\joinstate_0{=}\emptyset, \block_1,
\joinstate_1, \ldots, \block_m, \joinstate_m{=}\mathcal{E})$
decomposes the query into sequential block extensions.
The product $\prod_{j=1}^{m} \muFactor{\block_j}{\interface_j}$ is
a valid cardinality upper bound (the unit-weight $\ell_\infty$ case of Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}, so every result we use from~\cite{hceg2027} is restated and proved here).
Our framework generalizes this product by replacing $\ell_\infty$
norms with LP-optimized $\ell_p$-norms.

\subsection{LpBound: $\ell_p$-Norm Degree Sequences}

LpBound~\cite{zhang2025lpbound} uses $\ell_p$-norms of degree
sequences as tighter statistics.
For relation $\rel$ with degree sequence
$\mathbf{d} = (d_1, \ldots, d_k)$ where
$d_i = |\ext{\rel}{a_i}|$, the $\ell_p$-norm is
$\lpnorm{\mathbf{d}}{p} = (\sum_i d_i^p)^{1/p}$.
The core inequality is: for any $p \ge 1$,
\[
\tfrac{1}{p}\, h(X) + h(Y \mid X) \le \log \lpnorm{\mathbf{d}}{p}
\]
for the degree sequence $\mathbf{d}$ of $Y$ given $X$ in $\rel$.
Optimizing over all valid inequalities via an LP yields bounds
orders of magnitude tighter than max-degree alone.

\section{LpH-CEG Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{$\ell_p$-Norm Block Extension Factor}

\begin{definition}[$\ell_p$ Block Extension Factor]
\label{def:lp-mu}
For a block $\block$ with interface $\interface$, let
$\mathbf{d} = (d_1, \ldots, d_k)$ be the degree sequence where
$d_i = |\ext{\block}{a_i}|$ for each distinct interface value
$a_i \in \pi_\interface(\query_\block)$.
The $\ell_p$ block extension factor is:
\[
\muLp{p}{\block}{\interface} = \lpnorm{\mathbf{d}}{p}
= \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k} d_i^p\Bigr)^{1/p}
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent Special cases:
$\muLp{\infty}{\block}{\interface} = \max_i d_i = \muFactor{\block}{\interface}$ (max-degree);
$\muLp{1}{\block}{\interface} = \sum_i d_i = |\query_\block|$ (cardinality).

\subsection{Fractional Block Cover LP}

Given a hyperpath $P$ with $m$ steps, we define a variable
$w_{j,p} \ge 0$ for each step $j$ and each $p$-value.
The LP minimizes the log-space bound:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lp}
\min \sum_{j=1}^{m} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}}
w_{j,p} \cdot \log_2 \muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}
\end{equation}

subject to the fractional block cover constraints: for each
attribute $a \in V$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cover}
\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}}\;\Biggl(
\sum_{\substack{j:\, a \in \attr{\block_j} \setminus \interface_j}}
\!\!\! w_{j,p}
\;+\;
\sum_{\substack{j:\, a \in \interface_j}}
\frac{w_{j,p}}{p}
\Biggr)
\;\ge\; 1
\end{equation}

\subsection{Why Cyclic Queries Benefit}\label{sec:whycyclic}

\paragraph{Where the slack comes from}
With binary extensions every attribute is new at exactly one step. On a star each leaf is new at one step and never re-covered, so its cover forces $w_j{=}1$ and the LP returns the max-degree bound. On a path an interior attribute is
re-covered by the next interface, so the LP may trade part of an
extension for that interface: at $p{=}1$ an AGM-style bound (on a $4$-path,
weights $(1,0,1)$, i.e.\ $N_1N_3$), and on skewed data intermediate norms
too (on a $3$-path with one heavy value, adding $\ell_2$ lowers the bound
from $1{,}000$ to $330$; extended version). On a cycle the closing step re-covers attributes already bound, so
every attribute is covered by two steps; this is
the source of Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic}, a sufficient condition for the gain, not a necessary one.

\subsection{Algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:lph-ceg} summarizes the LpH-CEG estimation pipeline.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{LpH-CEG Estimation}
\label{alg:lph-ceg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Query hypergraph $H$, relation data $D$, block size limit $b$,
  $p$-values $\mathcal{P} = \{p_1, \ldots, p_r\}$
\ENSURE Upper bound $\hat{C}$ on $|\query(D)|$
\STATE Enumerate blocks $\mathcal{B} \gets \{F \subseteq \mathcal{E} :
  |F| \le b,\; F \text{ connected}\}$
\FOR{each block $F \in \mathcal{B}$ and interface $I$}
  \STATE Collect $\mathbf{d}_{F,I}$ \COMMENT{binary pairs: no join tuples}
  \STATE Compute $\muLp{p}{F}{I}$ for each $p \in \mathcal{P}$
\ENDFOR
\IF{$|V| \le 12$}
  \STATE \textbf{return} $2^{h^*}$, $h^* = \max h(V)$ over the Shannon cone under all $\ell_p$ block constraints
\ENDIF
\STATE Build block-extension DAG with $\ell_\infty$ weights
\STATE Find candidate hyperpaths $P_1, \ldots, P_K$ (shortest, then a bounded search) \COMMENT{$K{=}1$: default}
\FOR{each hyperpath $P_i = (F_1, \ldots, F_m)$}
  \STATE Solve LP~\eqref{eq:lp}--\eqref{eq:cover} for weights $(w^*_{j,p})$
  \STATE $\hat{C}_i \gets \prod_{j,p} \muLp{p}{F_j}{I_j}^{\,w^*_{j,p}}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{return} $\min(\hat{C}_1, \ldots, \hat{C}_K,\; \text{max-deg bound})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The LP in line~12 has $O(m \cdot |\mathcal{P}|)$ variables
($m \le |\mathcal{E}|$ steps, $|\mathcal{P}| = 3$ in practice) and $|V|$
constraints, solved by HiGHS~\cite{huangfu2018highs} in about $1$--$2$\,ms.
Lines~6--8 return the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}, the polymatroid program over block statistics (as \texttt{guaranteed\_block\_bound}; the flow LP is only a candidate checked against it). Beyond $|V|{=}12$, and as the instrument of Sections~\ref{sec:theory}--\ref{sec:vs-lpbound}, the cover-LP path (lines~9--15) is the guarantee; line~15 keeps it no looser than the max-degree bound.

\section{Theoretical Results}
\label{sec:theory}
Proofs not given in full here are in the extended version shipped with
the artifact (code, data pipeline, results:
\texttt{github.com/suanlab/LpH-CEG}).

\begin{theorem}[$\ell_p$ Composition Inequality]
\label{thm:composition}
Let $P = (\joinstate_0{=}\emptyset, \ldots, \joinstate_m{=}\mathcal{E})$
be a hyperpath, $\mathcal{P}\subseteq[1,\infty]$ (with $1/\infty{=}0$), and
let $(w_{j,p})$ satisfy the cover constraint~\eqref{eq:cover}, with the
statistics computed exactly on the database $D$. Then
\[
|\query| \le \prod_{j=1}^{m} \prod_{p \in \mathcal{P}}
\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}^{\,w_{j,p}}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $h$ be the entropy of the uniform distribution on $\query(D)$, so
$h(V)=\log_2|\query|$. Write $A_j=\attr{\block_j}$,
$V_j=\attr{\joinstate_j}$ and $E_j=A_j\setminus\interface_j$: the $E_j$
partition $V$ and $\interface_j\subseteq V_{j-1}$. Since
$\pi_{A_j}\query\subseteq\bowtie_{\rel\in\block_j}\rel$, the $\ell_p$
inequality of~\cite{abokhamis2024lpnorm} gives
$\frac1p h(\interface_j)+h(E_j\mid\interface_j)\le
\log_2\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}$. Weight by $w_{j,p}$ and sum; with
$w_j=\sum_p w_{j,p}$ and $s_j=\sum_p w_{j,p}/p$ it remains to show
$\sum_j[s_jh(\interface_j)+w_jh(E_j\mid\interface_j)]\ge h(V)$, where
\eqref{eq:cover} reads $w_{j(a)}+\sum_{j':a\in\interface_{j'}}s_{j'}\ge1$
for the step $j(a)$ that binds $a$. Let $g_j=h(E_j\mid V_{j-1})$ and
$\delta_j=\max(0,1-w_j)$. (i)~By the chain rule and submodularity,
$h(\interface_{j'})\ge\sum_j h(\interface_{j'}\cap E_j\mid V_{j-1})$.
(ii)~If $\delta_j>0$, \eqref{eq:cover} makes
$\{\interface_{j'}\cap E_j\}$, weighted $s_{j'}/\delta_j$, a fractional
cover of $E_j$; $S\mapsto h(S\mid V_{j-1})$ is monotone submodular, hence
fractionally subadditive, so
$\sum_{j'}s_{j'}h(\interface_{j'}\cap E_j\mid V_{j-1})\ge\delta_jg_j$.
(iii)~As $\interface_j\subseteq V_{j-1}$,
$w_jh(E_j\mid\interface_j)\ge(1-\delta_j)g_j$. Summing gives at least
$\sum_jg_j=h(V)$.
\end{proof}

Only monotonicity and submodularity are used, so the argument holds over
the whole polymatroid cone: the inequality is a Shannon-flow inequality~\cite{abokhamis2017panda}, here proved constructively for this
family. The redundant coverage that lets per-step
weights fall below $1$ (the source of every cyclic improvement, and the
reason the elementary per-step argument ($w_j\ge1$) does not apply) enters
exactly as the fractional cover in~(ii). The theorem assumes exact
statistics of the database being bounded; the sketched $\ell_2$ of
Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference} is outside it.

\paragraph{Independent checks} Two checks that do not rely on the proof
agree with it. Over $207{,}000$ checks (every database with nonempty relations over a two-value domain for the triangle and $4$-cycle, plus $15{,}000$ random ones across six shapes, each at block sizes $1$--$3$) the estimate never fell
below the exact count (minimum ratio $1.0$). And because the remaining
inequality is linear in $h$ and affine in $w$, an LP over the polymatroid
cone decides it for every vertex of the feasible weight region: across
$6{,}197$ hyperpath structures on eleven shapes with $|V|\le6$ and block
sizes $1$--$3$ ($1.73$M vertices; every hyperpath on $19$ of the $33$
(shape, block size) pairs) the minimum slack is $0$ to solver precision---tight, never negative. Reduced versions of both ship as regression tests.

\begin{theorem}[Subsumption]
\label{thm:subsumption}
LpH-CEG subsumes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Max-degree block bounds~\cite{hceg2027}: setting $p = \infty$,
  $w_j = 1$ recovers H-CEG's Composition Lemma.
\item Binary $\ell_p$-cover bounds: setting $|\block_j| = 1$ gives
  LP~\eqref{eq:lp}--\eqref{eq:cover} at $bs{=}1$---a polynomial-size
  relaxation of LpBound's~\cite{zhang2025lpbound} entropic LP
  (Section~\ref{sec:vs-lpbound}), not necessarily its entropic optimum.
\item AGM bound~\cite{atserias2008agm}: with $|\block_j|{=}1$ and $p{=}1$ on every step,
  each attribute's coverage is $1$ per covering step, so~\eqref{eq:cover}
  becomes the fractional edge-cover constraint and the objective becomes
  $\prod_j |\rel_j|^{w_j}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Relationship to LpBound}
\label{sec:vs-lpbound}

LpBound~\cite{zhang2025lpbound} also applies to cyclic queries and also
solves a linear program over $\ell_p$-norm degree statistics, so we must
delineate precisely what the block-cover LP adds.

\paragraph{Two different LPs}
LpBound's bound is the optimum of an \emph{entropic} LP: it maximizes
$h(V)$ over the Shannon cone (monotonicity and submodularity
inequalities on all $2^{|V|}$ entropic terms) subject to the
$\ell_p$-norm statistics constraints~\cite{zhang2025lpbound,abokhamis2024lpnorm}.
We follow LpBound in calling it the entropic bound, although the Shannon
cone is the polymatroid relaxation of the entropic cone, so it is the
strongest bound LpBound computes from those statistics, not necessarily
the tightest they admit.
Our LP~\eqref{eq:lp}--\eqref{eq:cover} instead optimizes
$O(m\,|\mathcal{P}|)$ weights over a \emph{fixed hyperpath}, with one
fractional-cover constraint per attribute.
On identical statistics the entropic optimum is the tighter of the two by
construction, and we do not claim otherwise: at $bs{=}1$ the cover bound
is $1.25$--$1.43\times$ looser on synthetic power-law cycles
(Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}), $1.9$--$3.5\times$ on six hand-picked
real Hetionet cycles, and up to $8.8\times$ on a cycle-$8$ repeating one relation, where the entropic cone exploits
conditional structure the fixed hyperpath treats independently.

\paragraph{On a fixed hyperpath, reweighting is exhausted}
Could a better weighting of the hyperpath's statistics close that gap?
No: the cover constraint is also necessary.

\begin{proposition}[Necessity]
\label{prop:necessity}
If nonnegative weights $(w_{j,p})$ make the product of
Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} an upper bound on $|\query(D)|$ for every database $D$ whose atoms' relations are independent (no self-joins), they satisfy~\eqref{eq:cover}. So on a fixed hyperpath the
LP~\eqref{eq:lp}--\eqref{eq:cover} is optimal over all weightings of its
statistics that are valid on every database.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $a\in V$ and $N\ge2$, and let $D_N$ give $a$ the domain $[N]$ and
every other attribute a single value. Then $|\query(D_N)|=N$, and
$\muLp{p}{\block_j}{\interface_j}$ is $N^{1/p}$ if $a\in\interface_j$, $N$
if $a\in A_j\setminus\interface_j$, and $1$ otherwise. The bound is
$N^{c_a}$, with $c_a$ the left side of~\eqref{eq:cover}, and
$N^{c_a}\ge N$ forces $c_a\ge1$.
\end{proof}

\noindent (In entropy terms, $D_N$ is the modular polymatroid $h(S)=[a\in S]\log_2N$. With self-joins the copies' identity admits other valid weights, e.g.\ $|R|^3$ for a triangle on one relation $R$, which leaves an attribute uncovered.) A cutting-plane search with the polymatroid LP
confirms this optimality on triangle, cycle-$4$/$5$/$6$ and $K_4$ (cycle-4: $15.335$
in $\log_2$, against $14.858$ for the polymatroid optimum). The residual
gap comes from \emph{statistics} the hyperpath leaves unused, which the
polymatroid program exploits and Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}
extends.

\paragraph{Efficiency}
The Shannon-cone \emph{formulation} of that optimum has $2^{|V|}$
variables and $O(2^{|V|}|V|^2)$ constraints and degrades accordingly:
$81$\,ms at $|V|{=}8$, $25$\,s at $|V|{=}12$, $41$\,min at $|V|{=}14$,
unsolvable beyond (Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}; the same curve on real Hetionet degree sequences: $22$\,s and $27$\,min). But that is not what
LpBound solves. Its LPflow~\cite[Thm.~4.5]{zhang2025lpbound} rewrites
every simple $\ell_p$ constraint (one conditioning attribute) as a flow
bundle after Im et al.~\cite{im2025simple} and is polynomial; our
reimplementation returns the entropic bound to $10^{-6}$ on every
instance we compared ($12$ family--seed pairs, the sweep, the $240$ long-suite queries) in $3$--$26$\,ms at $k{=}3$--$16$
(Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}). So at $bs{=}1$ the cover LP buys
\emph{no} asymptotic advantage over LpBound: as a $bs{=}1$ bound it is
dominated (looser, and both solve in milliseconds). The fixed hyperpath buys the per-step decomposition
(item~1 below) and the framework the statistics vocabulary (item~2), where
inference becomes non-trivial again (Section~\ref{sec:scalability}).

\paragraph{What the block-cover LP adds}
What LpBound's single global program does not provide is structural:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A per-step residual bound.} Because the LP is attached to
  a hyperpath, it yields a bound \emph{per extension step}; re-solved with a
  variable prefix projected out, it bounds the residual for every prefix
  value, the signal our WCOJ ordering consumes (Section~\ref{sec:wcoj}).
  LpBound could be re-solved likewise but has no per-step decomposition.
\item \textbf{Block-level statistics.} With multi-way blocks
  ($|\block_j| > 1$) the LP draws on $\ell_p$-norms of \emph{joined}
  block relations---statistics outside LpBound's single-relation
  vocabulary.
\end{enumerate}
So at $bs{=}1$ LpH-CEG trades tightness for a decomposable bound (which our WCOJ ordering uses, without beating max-degree ordering in general; Section~\ref{sec:wcoj}); item~2 is the axis the paper is about
(Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).

\begin{theorem}[Cyclic Improvement]
\label{thm:cyclic}
Consider a cycle query $C_k$ ($k \ge 3$) evaluated by binary
extensions in cycle order, where relation $R_j$ has cardinality
$N_j = |R_j|$, maximum degree $M_j$, and $\ell_p$-norms $\mu_p(R_j)$ of
its degree sequence from $a_j$ to $a_{j+1}$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{(Standard bound.)} The max-degree bound is
\[
B_{\max} = N_1 \prod_{j=2}^{k-1} M_j ,
\]
where the first extension has empty interface and contributes the
cardinality $N_1$, the $k-2$ \emph{middle} extensions each contribute
a max-degree $M_j$, and the cycle-closing extension (interface of size
two, empty extension set) contributes a factor of $1$.
\item \textbf{(LP bound.)} For every $p \in \mathcal{P}$ (with $1 \in \mathcal{P}$) the fractional
block-cover LP~\eqref{eq:lp}--\eqref{eq:cover} is feasible with weight
$p/(p{+}1)$ and exponent $p$ on every middle extension, together with
the split $(\frac{p}{p+1}, \frac{1}{p+1})$ of unit weight across the first
and closing ($\ell_1$) extensions. This yields
\[
B_{\mathrm{LP}} \le \min_{p \in \mathcal{P}}\; N_1^{\frac{p}{p+1}} N_k^{\frac{1}{p+1}}
\prod_{j=2}^{k-1} \mu_p(R_j)^{\frac{p}{p+1}} ,
\]
e.g.\ $N_1^{2/3} N_k^{1/3} \prod_j \mu_2(R_j)^{2/3}$ at $p{=}2$. (Within this family the exponent must be common, or non-increasing along the cycle: the cover
constraint of the attribute between edges $j$ and $j{+}1$ reads
$\frac{p_j}{p_j+1} + \frac{1}{p_{j+1}+1} \ge 1$.)
\item \textbf{(Improvement.)} Therefore, for every $p \in \mathcal{P}$,
\[
\frac{B_{\max}}{B_{\mathrm{LP}}} \;\ge\;
\Bigl(\frac{N_1}{N_k}\Bigr)^{\!\frac{1}{p+1}}
\prod_{j=2}^{k-1}
\frac{M_j}{\mu_p(R_j)^{\,p/(p+1)}} .
\]
At $p{=}2$ a middle factor exceeds $1$ iff $\mu_2(R_j)^2 < M_j^3$: the
sum of squared degrees is below the cube of the maximum degree, as when
a few high-degree values carry the relation ($n$ values of uniform degree
$d$ qualify iff $n<d$; many degree-$1$ values do not). As $p \to \infty$
every factor tends to $1$.
The boundary factor is a constant independent of $k$;
for a \emph{uniform} cycle ($N_j \equiv N$) it equals $1$, and in the
general case it is $\ge 1$ for a hyperpath entering the cycle at the
largest relation ($N_1 \ge N_k$). If every middle factor is at least
$c > 1$, the ratio is at least $c^{\,k-2}(N_1/N_k)^{1/(p+1)}$: it grows
geometrically in the number of middle edges. Since LpH-CEG returns
$\min(B_{\max}, B_{\mathrm{LP}})$, the realized ratio is always
$\ge 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The standard bound (1) follows directly from the cycle hyperpath
structure: the first extension has empty interface, so its degree
sequence is the single value $N_1$ and $\mu_\infty = N_1$; each middle
extension adds one attribute over a size-one interface with
$\mu_\infty = M_j$; the closing extension has both endpoints already in
the state, so its conditional degrees are all $1$ and $\mu_\infty = 1$.
For (2), each attribute is covered as an extension in one step (rate
$1$) and as an interface in the next (rate $1/p$); with a common $p$ the
local constraint $w + w/p \ge 1$ is tight at $w = p/(p+1)$. Attribute
$a_2$ then reads $w_1 + \frac{1}{p+1} \ge 1$, $a_k$ reads
$\frac{p}{p+1} + w_k \ge 1$ and $a_1$ reads $w_1 + w_k \ge 1$, all tight
at $w_1 = \frac{p}{p+1}$, $w_k = \frac{1}{p+1}$. The closing step has
$\mu_1 = N_k$, so the first/closing contributions are
$N_1^{p/(p+1)} N_k^{1/(p+1)}$ against $N_1$ in $B_{\max}$. The LP optimum
is at most every such point, and may mix $p$ further.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Observation} On power-law cycles with
fixed parameters the middle factors exceed $1$, so the bound predicts a
ratio geometric in $k$; the measured one grows accordingly (10-seed means): $8.4\times$ ($k{=}4$), $69\times$
($k{=}6$), $614\times$ ($k{=}8$).

\subsection{Tightness}

Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} establishes that LpH-CEG bounds are
\emph{valid} (never underestimate), but not necessarily \emph{tight}
(matching a worst-case instance).
Tightness depends on the degree sequence structure:

Each step's $\ell_p$ inequality is attained for any
interface (by the distribution $P(u)\propto d_u^p$ with a uniform
conditional).

Per-step tightness does not make the \emph{composition} tight: the
weighted product can be loose even where every factor is exact, and we
measure it to be $1.25$--$1.43\times$ looser than the entropic optimum at
$bs{=}1$ on synthetic cycles and a median $2.7$--$6.8\times$ on the real long suites, $59\times$ on G-CARE's AIDS (Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).

Block size is a trade-off: binary extensions keep simple
interfaces; multi-way blocks capture block-level correlation at the cost
of non-simple ones. Results use $bs{=}1$ unless marked $bs{=}2$.

\subsection{Delta over the Block Framework}

We inherit the block-extension DAG and hyperpath machinery from
H-CEG~\cite{hceg2027}, which uses only $\ell_\infty$ weights with no
optimization layer. What this paper adds is the $\ell_p$ fractional cover
LP that replaces those fixed scalars, the separation of the statistics and
inference axes (Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}), the flow-LP
inference over block statistics (Section~\ref{sec:scalability}), and
the per-step residual signal; none exists in H-CEG. Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic}
additionally supplies a structural test neither predecessor provides,
lower-bounding the magnitude by a product of $k-2$ per-edge factors
$M_j / \mu_p(\rel_j)^{p/(p+1)}$ at a common $p$.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
All experiments run on one server (Intel Xeon Gold 5218R, $20$ cores, $251$\,GiB RAM; SciPy~1.15 with HiGHS, DuckDB~1.5.4); worker counts are stated where runs were concurrent.

\paragraph{Datasets}
We evaluate on five real-world datasets:
\textbf{Hetionet}~\cite{himmelstein2017hetionet} (biomedical knowledge graph; $13$ metaedges, $20$K nodes, $438$K edges);
\textbf{Epinions} (social network, 75K nodes, 509K edges);
\textbf{IMDb}, JOB's snapshot~\cite{leis2015goodare}: the $200$
most-voted feature films for a cycle suite, and the JOB-medium subset;
and the AIDS and YAGO graphs of G-CARE~\cite{park2020gcare} with its
cyclic queries.
We also use synthetic power-law graphs (40--5{,}000 nodes, 120--20{,}000 edges).
Seeds and runs are stated per table: multi-seed results are 10-seed
means; single-instance tables say so.

\paragraph{Queries}
Synthetic cycle-$k$ for $k \in \{3,\ldots,16\}$ plus diamond and $K_4$ families;
on real data, three rule-selected, hash-pinned suites
(Section~\ref{sec:suite}) plus a handful of hand-picked cycles.

\paragraph{Baselines}
AGM (fractional edge cover, $\prod |R_i|^{w_i}$);
CEG$_M$ (binary max-degree shortest path, $bs{=}1$; equivalent to MOLP on binary queries~\cite{joglekar2016molp,chen2022ceg});
LpH-CEG (LP-optimized $\ell_p$-norms,
$\mathcal{P} = \{1,2,\infty\}$). Every program in a comparison gets the
same $\mathcal{P}$, including our entropic LpBound. Because the entropic
bound gains more from extra norms than the cover LP does, the comparisons
against LpBound (the abstract, Table~\ref{tab:long-suite}'s
upper block, the short suite, Tables~\ref{tab:stats-vs-inf} and~\ref{tab:gcare}) use
the eleven norms $\mathcal{P}=\{1,\ldots,10,\infty\}$ on both sides; every other comparison
with the entropic bound (the other synthetic tables and figures, the six
hand-picked cycles, the CorrBound proxy, the $370$-query suite) uses three
norms, $\{1,2,\infty\}$.
The ``LpH-CEG'' column throughout denotes our
\emph{cover}-LP at $bs{=}1$ (Section~\ref{sec:vs-lpbound}); it is
\emph{not} the entropic LpBound of
Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025lpbound}, against which we compare
separately in Section~\ref{sec:vs-lpbound} (our cover bound is
$1.25$--$1.43\times$ looser on synthetic cycles, a median
$2.7$--$6.8\times$ on Hetionet and IMDb).
Learned and summary estimators (FactorJoin, GNCE,
COLOR~\cite{wu2023factorjoin,schwabe2024gnce,deeds2025color}) produce \emph{point}
estimates without guaranteed upper bounds; CorrBound, which enriches the
entropic program with cross-relation statistics, is compared on its own workload and through a proxy (Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).

\paragraph{Unified bound comparison}
On one synthetic suite (table in the extended version) the four guaranteed-bound methods order as predicted: CEG$_M$, AGM $\ge$ Cover-LP $\ge$ Entropic
(Theorem~\ref{thm:subsumption}), with AGM and CEG$_M$ incomparable. Our cover-LP is
$2.3$--$35.7\times$ tighter than CEG$_M$ on synthetic cycles, is also
consistently tighter than AGM (e.g.\ $2.0\times$ on cycle-6), and stays
within $1.25$--$1.36\times$ of the entropic (polymatroid) optimum on the
same single-relation $\ell_p$ statistics on these cycle-$3$--$6$ instances (one instance per cycle).

schema in which every relation joins movies to people, companies or
keywords) and separates the axes only with a program that can use the
statistics. With eleven norms the inference gap is $5.1$--$6.8\times$, and
$bs{=}2$ statistics are only $1.3$/$2.9$/$6.8\times$ tighter than the
entropic bound under the cover LP (level with or below that gap, and
above the entropic bound on $8$ of the $40$ queries at $|V|{=}6$), but
$9.7$/$30$/$87\times$ under the polymatroid program, on $120/120$. On
these statistics it beats the cover LP by $7.6$--$11.6\times$, against
$5.1$--$6.8\times$ at $bs{=}1$: richer statistics reopen the program here
too. Under it the bound moves from $23$--$1{,}300\times$ the
truth to $2.2$--$15\times$ ($101$ checked queries, exact counts
$3{\cdot}10^{5}$--$9{\cdot}10^{7}$ at the median; $19$ of the $80$ at
$|V|{=}8$--$10$ timed out; these are the largest, so the ratios to the
truth describe the smaller queries). With three norms the entropic baseline is
$3.5$--$6.7\times$ looser and the cover-LP margin reads
$4.0$/$13$/$43\times$. Pair-join sizes barely help (three norms: median
$1.00\times$; they help at all on $24/120$, by at most $1.35\times$): half
the pairs share an entity and so join one relation with itself, with
size $\sum_x \deg(x)^2$, that relation's $\ell_2^2$, which LpBound already
has; the movie-sharing pairs are genuine inner products but rarely bind.
The interface-conditioned statistics still add $17$--$171\times$. (IMDb
has no catalog run; the catalog and its $7$--$10$\,ms
estimate are Hetionet's.) At five times the films (exploratory, bounds only) every tightness ratio grows and collection by materialized joins grows faster than the data ($48$\,s median per query, up to $25$\,min and $16$\,GiB on a shared machine; extended version), by sparse products less so ($9.3$\,s, $83$\,s, $0.9$\,GB; Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).

\emph{G-CARE.} As an external check we run every cyclic query of the
G-CARE benchmark~\cite{park2020gcare} on its AIDS and YAGO graphs ($241$
and $366$; query sets fixed by G-CARE, size caps below ours), with
labelled, directed edges as binary relations filtered by the query's
vertex labels, homomorphism counts, and eleven norms on both sides
(Table~\ref{tab:gcare}). Our exact counts equal G-CARE's on $241/241$ AIDS
queries; on YAGO they differ on $49$ of the $192$ we evaluate, in both
directions and under every semantics we tried (directed or not, injective
or not; over all $364$ cyclic YAGO queries with a count, $80$ differ). A DuckDB-free reimplementation reproduces our count on all $49$, so the gap is in semantics or preprocessing we could not reconstruct and is unsettled (extended version). We check validity against our counts (one YAGO bound, $26$, lies
below G-CARE's $33$ but above our $17$). $174$ YAGO queries exceed the
prototype (a relation over $300$K rows or a pair join over $20$M) and are
reported, not run. Under the polymatroid program, block statistics are
$15\times$ (AIDS) and $24\times$ (YAGO) tighter than LpBound and never looser; the YAGO median mixes $12\times$ on the $143$ queries whose counts agree with G-CARE's and $311\times$ on the $49$ that do not, and inclusion and gain vary by topology (extended version). Under
the cover LP the inference gap dominates on AIDS: $59\times$, plausibly as bounded
molecular degrees make high norms very informative to the entropic
program, against a $1.35\times$ statistics gain (block statistics beat
LpBound on only $135/241$); on YAGO the two are comparable ($6.1\times$
against $8.9\times$). With three norms the AIDS order flips ($3.4\times$ against $21\times$). On both
graphs the $bs{=}2$ program gap ($12\times$, $2.7\times$) is below the
$bs{=}1$ one. G-CARE is thus a regime where the program matters as much as the statistics.

\paragraph{Which statistic needs the join}
Of the eight interfaces of a block of binary relations $R(a,b),S(b,c)$,
seven (five non-trivial) come from a scan of one relation with degree lookups in the other
(the degree of $a$ is $\sum_{b\in N_R(a)}\deg_S(b)$, that of $b$ is
$\deg_R(b)\deg_S(b)$); only $\{a,c\}$, the common-neighbour count
$|N_R(a)\cap N_S(c)|$, needs the pair join, and only its adjacency: one
sparse product $A_RA_S$, not the join tuples. Its $\ell_2$ is determined
neither by the other interfaces nor by shared-attribute inner products
(extended version), while its $\ell_1$ is the join size. In the headline
setting (eleven norms, polymatroid), the pair sizes and the pairwise
products $\sum_b \deg_R(b)^q\deg_S(b)^r$ of interfaces $\emptyset$, $\{b\}$,
$\{a,b\}$, $\{b,c\}$, $\{a,b,c\}$ ($(q,r)\in\{(1,1),(p,p),(1,p),(p,1)\}$: an exact proxy for CorrBound's inner products, not its full vocabulary)
gain a median $42\times$ (Hetionet) and $2.0\times$ (IMDb) over LpBound; the
one-side and common-neighbour statistics add a further $34\times$ and
$14\times$ on $120/120$ queries, and alone reach $1{,}674\times$ and
$28.7\times$ (full: $1{,}724\times$, $29.4\times$). The join-free seven
carry $88\%$ and $86\%$ of the log-gain, the common-neighbour $\ell_2$
leaves $1.47\times$ and $1.39\times$ (full on $1/120$ each), and with three
norms and the flow LP it alone restores the full bound on $85/120$ and
$116/120$ (details, and a sketch-guided choice of blocks, in the extended
version).

\subsection{Real Data Results}
\label{sec:realdata}

At $bs{=}1$ on real data the LP fires only where cyclic structure meets
cross-relation degree asymmetry (Section~\ref{sec:noimprove}). The
heterogeneous Hetionet cycle-4, four relation types with asymmetric
degrees, improves $7.59\times$ ($53$M${\to}7.0$M, relations capped at
$30$K edges); the C-D-G and D-G-A Hetionet triangles and the replicated Epinions
cycles on $2$--$5$K-edge file-order prefixes yield exactly $1.00\times$ (at $20$K
edges, in the registered suite below, the Epinions cycle-4 improves $2.06\times$).
Over six closed metapaths of length $3$--$5$
built from Hetionet's thirteen edge types, LpH-CEG fires
\emph{selectively}: it tightens C-G-G
($1.95\times$), C-D-G-G and C-D-A-G-G ($7.59\times$)---exactly those
whose hyperpath places the skewed gene--gene relation in a \emph{middle}
position---and returns $1.00\times$ on the rest. The effect is
\emph{structural}: D-G-G-A holds the same high-skew relation as C-D-G-G
yet yields $1.00\times$, in our reading because it sits on the closing edge, where Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} has no middle factor.

\emph{Beyond cycles.} On a diamond (also run as a chordal 4-cycle, the same shape) and a $4$-clique over the same edges, LpH-CEG improves $1.9$--$4.2\times$ over max-degree (largest on the $4$-clique), so the effect is not confined to cycles.

\emph{A pre-registered suite.}\label{sec:suite} A handful of hand-chosen queries cannot
support a distributional claim, so we fixed a suite \emph{before}
measuring: every type-consistent metagraph cycle of length $3$--$5$ over
distinct Hetionet relations ($370$ queries, admitted by rule), plus seven
Epinions shapes. The registry is hash-pinned and the evaluation refuses
to run against an edited copy, so the selection could not be changed unnoticed after measuring. All $377$ were evaluated and \emph{none} censored. Over the
$370$ Hetionet queries the cover/entropic gap has median $1.99\times$,
P90 $3.55\times$, maximum $6.29\times$ ($215$ distinct values), meeting
the median ${\le}3\times$, P90 ${\le}8\times$ bar registered in advance.
Improvement over max-degree has median $1.47\times$ and reaches
$13.1\times$, yet $112/370$ queries ($30\%$) improve by exactly
$1.00\times$---the selective behavior above, now over a population
rather than a handful. The Epinions part is degenerate and we report it
so: one homogeneous relation self-joined makes all seven shapes yield an
identical cover/entropic \emph{gap} of $3.28\times$---one distinct
observation rather than seven. A second registry extends the rule to
lengths $6$, $8$ and $10$, where the $777$/$4{,}380$/$12{,}960$ admissible
cycles are too many to solve entropically, so the first $40$ per length in
SHA-256 order of the canonical relation sequence are admitted (an order
blind to data and bounds), giving the $120$ Hetionet queries of
Table~\ref{tab:long-suite}. A third registry, fixed after a disclosed
pilot, uses IMDb from the Join Order Benchmark~\cite{leis2015goodare}:
the $200$ most-voted feature films ($197$--$37$K rows per relation), with
one relation for each of nine of the twelve cast roles (costume and
production designers and guests omitted when the relations were defined,
before any bound was computed), for distributors, for
production companies, and for keywords. Each relation joins movies to an
entity type of its own, so a person reached as an actor and as a director
are different variables: cross-role joins are excluded by design. The
schema is then a star---a cycle alternates movies with entities, each
entity reached by one relation on both sides, and relations repeat (JOB
joins \texttt{movie\_companies} and \texttt{title} twice); of the
$364$/$3{,}081$/$25{,}752$ such cycles at length $6$/$8$/$10$ the first
$40$ in hash order are admitted. The pilot used the Hetionet rule (one
person type, distinct relations) on the first $3{,}500$ feature films of
the year 2005; $82/120$ of its joins were empty, so validity could not be
tested, and scope and typing were both changed before registering. The
pilot favored block statistics more (median cover $bs{=}2$/entropic
$9.5{\cdot}10^{-6}$ against $0.077$ here); it, and a run at five times
the films (Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}), are in the artifact.


\subsection{Scalability}
\label{sec:scalability}

On power-law cycles ($100$V/$400$E, median of 7 runs) the
LP adds under $4.7$\,ms to the max-degree bound ($1.17$--$1.34\times$
overhead, $8$--$24$\,ms in total for $k{=}3$--$10$) while the
improvement grows exponentially with $k$, reaching $971\times$ at
cycle-10.

\paragraph{Polynomial inference on block statistics}
The Shannon-cone formulation of the entropic bound has $2^{|V|}$
variables and, on the power-law cycles of Figure~\ref{fig:crossover},
rises from $81$\,ms at $k{=}8$ to $25$\,s at $k{=}12$ and $2{,}480$\,s
at $k{=}14$ (real Hetionet sequences: $22$\,s and $27$\,min), timed end
to end with a sparse constraint matrix and HiGHS's interior-point solver. LpBound does not solve that program. Its LPflow~\cite[Thm.~4.5]{zhang2025lpbound}
follows Im et al.~\cite{im2025simple}: each \emph{simple} constraint
($|X| \le 1$) becomes a capacitated edge $X \to Y$ in a flow network
over attribute sets (an $\ell_p$ constraint adds $\emptyset \to X$ at
capacity $\delta/p$), and the bound is the cheapest capacity assignment
that routes one unit of flow to every attribute---a polynomial LP. Our
reimplementation returns the entropic bound to $10^{-6}$ on all $12$
instances of Table~\ref{tab:stats-vs-inf} and at every $k \le 14$
here, in $3$--$26$\,ms for $k{=}3$--$16$; the cover LP is
$1.25$--$1.43\times$ looser than a program that is itself polynomial;
both solve in milliseconds.

The question that remains open is inference over \emph{block}
statistics, where a $bs{=}2$ block over three attributes contributes
constraints conditioned on two of them ($|X|{=}2$)---the non-simple
case, which Im et al.\ show to be as hard as the general polymatroid
bound already at $|X| \le 2$, $|Y| \le 3$ (their Theorem~1.3(c)). Their
\S7 relaxation handles such a constraint along a fixed attribute order
$\pi$ by restricting it to the attributes of $Y$ that follow all of $X$;
we carry the $\ell_p$ term through unchanged. The relaxed constraint is
implied by the original (monotonicity), and for $\ell_\infty$ constraints
every order's flow bound is valid by their Theorem~1.5(a); for $\ell_p$
we have no proof and certify each bound instead (numerically, to the solver's tolerance, not in exact arithmetic). The $2^{|V|}$
polymatroid bound over the \emph{unrelaxed} constraints is valid for
every $p$ (each $\ell_p$ constraint holds on the join's entropy, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}), so a flow bound at or above it is valid; a flow bound counts as a guarantee only with this certificate. The released \texttt{guaranteed\_block\_bound} therefore returns the polymatroid optimum up to $|V|{=}12$ and the cover LP on the same statistics beyond (Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}; the sweep's $k{=}16$), keeping the flow value only as a candidate.
Every real-data flow bound passes (eleven norms: $109/109$ short,
$240/240$ long-suite, $433/433$ G-CARE and $80/80$ resampled slice
queries, equal on $633$, to $10^{-6}$), as do the synthetic sweep's at
$k{=}12$ and $14$ (equal; $k{=}16$, where the $2^{|V|}$ program does not
finish, is uncertified) and $40$ random connected shapes ($|V| \le 5$, all
$2{,}046$ order--instance pairs, also never below the exact join). Two facts make the
relaxation usable. The
$|X|{=}2$ constraints carry most of the $bs{=}2$ gain (dropping them
loosens the polymatroid bound $1.5$--$2.9\times$), and an order keeps
one exactly when both conditioning attributes precede its target---so on
a cycle an \emph{alternating} order (one color class, then the other)
keeps every constraint centered on the second class; no single order
keeps them all, so we solve the flow LP over the $4|V|$ walk and
alternating orders and keep the best bound. Over the full $bs{=}2$ catalog
(every block, every interface) it equals the $2^{|V|}$ polymatroid bound
on $12/12$ family--seed pairs (three norms; $7/12$ with eleven) and on every synthetic even cycle up to $k{=}14$ ($24$\,min for the $2^{|V|}$ program on this catalog at $k{=}14$) and on
the triangle,
and loses a constant $1.56$--$1.59\times$ on cycle-$5$/$7$, where one
wedge must remain lost;
$34/40$ of the random shapes are exact (max gap $1.28\times$). Cost is
$4$--$73$\,ms per order and $0.03$--$4.4$\,s in total at $k{=}3$--$16$
(Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}). What it \emph{finds} matters more than what
it costs: the cover LP, confined to one hyperpath, leaves most of the
block statistics unused, and the flow LP over the same catalog is
$1.5$--$3.8\times$ tighter on the small families (eleven norms:
$1.6$--$3.8\times$) and, with three norms, $2.2\times$ at $k{=}3$ growing
to $288\times$ at $k{=}16$ (uncertified) on the sweep (below the $bs{=}1$ optimum by
$2$--$800\times$ where the cover LP manages $1$--$6\times$;
Figure~\ref{fig:positioning}). On real data the $2^{|V|}$ program over
the same catalog is cheap enough to solve directly (eleven norms: median
$0.02$/$0.18$/$2.4$\,s at $|V|{=}6$/$8$/$10$ on Hetionet, and $63$\,s for the slowest, a $|V|{=}12$ G-CARE query), so the eleven-norm real-data bounds we
report are its optimum. The flow LP equals it on $633$ of the $862$ reported
queries and is within $2.41\times$ on all; it is the route beyond
$|V|{\approx}12$. On the $120$-query Hetionet long suite the median gain
of the polymatroid program over the cover LP on the same statistics is
$5.0/6.7/11.1\times$ at $|V|{=}6/8/10$; no bound falls below the exact
count.

\paragraph{Choice of $p$-values}
On that suite's cycle-6, $\mathcal{P} = \{1,\infty\}$ (2 values) already
achieves $17.6\times$ improvement; adding $\ell_2$ ($\mathcal{P} =
\{1,2,\infty\}$) reaches $35.7\times$; further values ($\ell_3$,
$\ell_5$) yield no additional gain.
We recommend $\mathcal{P} = \{1, 2, \infty\}$ as the default (cardinality
$\ell_1$, variance $\ell_2$, worst case $\ell_\infty$); repeating the
sweep on four families (cycle-4/6, $K_4$, chordal-5; extended version) confirms this is not
cycle-specific---$\ell_2$ helps in every family and $p$ beyond
$\{1,2,\infty\}$ adds $<2.4\%$ to the cover LP. This does not hold for
the entropic bound, which gains a median $1.9$--$6.7\times$ from eleven norms
on the real suites; comparisons against it therefore use eleven norms on
both sides (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).



\subsection{Bound-Guided Join Ordering}
\label{sec:robustness}\label{sec:wcoj}

\emph{Binary ordering in an engine.} (Greedy WCOJ ordering by the per-step residuals is $1.3\%$ worse at the median over $66$ queries; extended version.) We load each query into DuckDB, compute a
left-deep order by minimizing the classic $C_{\mathrm{out}}$
cost~\cite{cluet1995complexity} (sum of the bound's estimated intermediate
cardinalities), inject it by disabling DuckDB's join-order optimizer, and
time real execution. As a reference we also run DuckDB's own optimizer
(its cardinality estimates come from table statistics such as
HyperLogLog distinct counts).

Two findings frame the result. A cardinality \emph{upper bound} is a
conservative cost model, so ranking plans by it need not match an engine
optimizer on average (over all left-deep orders the cover LP also
correlates less with true intermediate cost than the entropic bound:
Kendall $\tau$ $0.27$--$0.87$ against $0.33$--$0.94$); and on a single
cycle every proper sub-query is acyclic, where the cyclic slack of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} is absent (intermediate norms can still help there,
Section~\ref{sec:whycyclic}), so that theorem's gain reaches the ranking only
through \emph{intermediate} sub-queries that are themselves cyclic (cliques, chordal cycles).

What the pessimistic bound \emph{does} buy is robustness against
catastrophic plans. Table~\ref{tab:robust} reports, per query, each
strategy's slowdown over the best plan we found. DuckDB's optimizer is
excellent on average (often faster than any forced left-deep order
because it builds bushy plans), but on dense $4$-cliques its
statistics-based estimates badly underestimate the cyclic intermediate
blow-up, and it runs $7$--$21\times$ slower than the best evaluated plan across every clique density we tried. Bound-guided ordering, whose estimates never fall below that blow-up, stays within $3.6\times$ of the best evaluated plan (${\le}25$ sampled orders) on
\emph{every} query in the suite. The worst-case (tail) slowdown is
$3.6\times$ for bound-guided versus $21\times$ for the engine---a
$5.9\times$ heavier tail for the engine's optimizer.

(cycle-3/4, $K_4$, diamond; cycle-5 and chordal-5 censored entirely, by
the AGM bound, which depends only on relation sizes, so that our own
bound cannot select its favorable cases). Medians are close
($1.03\times$ vs.\ $0.93\times$) while the tails diverge: the
bound-guided P95/max are $1.21\times$/$1.31\times$ against DuckDB's
$5.82\times$/$8.17\times$ (with $n{=}24$, P95 is the third-largest
value). The reference is the best \emph{forced left-deep} order, so
bound-guided is ${\ge}1$ by construction while DuckDB, free to go bushy, scores below $1$ on median in this single-run measurement: a bound-guided choice avoids a plan space's tail; it does not beat the engine. Five shuffled repetitions per plan keep the tail (P95/max $1.18$/$1.21\times$ against $5.51$/$6.77\times$; per repetition P95 ${\le}1.65\times$ against ${\ge}5.07\times$; medians $1.03$ and $1.18\times$), and so does a raw-logged replication that picks the reference on two runs and measures on four (P95 $1.11$ against $5.26\times$). The tail is shape-dependent: DuckDB's plan is faster than the bound-guided one on triangles and 4-cycles (median ratio $1.13$, $1.78$) and slower on diamonds and $K_4$ ($0.53$, $0.25$; extended version).

\paragraph{Real data, block statistics} The same protocol on the $40$
IMDb cycles at $|V|{=}6$, with four estimators behind $C_{\mathrm{out}}$,
tests the thesis rather than the $bs{=}1$ bound. Block statistics move the
choice toward the true-cardinality order: the flow LP on $bs{=}2$ picks
the order exact counts would pick on $24/40$ queries, and on the $33$
whose forced orders all finish within $60$\,s its median slowdown over the
best timed order (the strategies' picks plus $16$ sampled, of the $96$
connected left-deep orders) is $1.19\times$, against $1.41\times$ for the
$bs{=}1$ cover LP, $1.23\times$ for LpBound (three norms) and $1.21\times$ for exact
counts---differences among the last three within noise. None competes with DuckDB's optimizer here ($0.43\times$
median, at most $2.0\times$): IMDb's cycles do not trigger its tail, and
on the other $7$ queries almost every forced left-deep order exceeds
$60$\,s while DuckDB finishes, so excluding them favors the forced
orders. Each order was timed once while $10$ queries ran concurrently, so
``within noise'' is a judgment, not a test. A better bound improves
\emph{which} left-deep plan is chosen, not whether one should be.

LpH-CEG is thus not a replacement for a
well-tuned optimizer, but a guide that limits the worst case on the
dense cyclic queries that defeat its estimates (on our synthetic cliques,
not on IMDb), consistent with the pessimistic-estimation rationale of avoiding catastrophic plans~\cite{cai2019pessimistic}.

\subsection{When LpH-CEG Does Not Help}
\label{sec:noimprove}

On our workloads LpH-CEG yields no improvement ($1.00\times$) on
(1)~acyclic star queries (no redundant coverage) and (2)~cycles of replicated relations with
near-symmetric sampled degrees (the $5$K-edge-prefix Epinions cycles,
Section~\ref{sec:realdata}, and all $8$ IMDb co-actor/co-director and
replicated movie--actor cycle-$4$ instances at $5$K--$1$M rows); case~(2) is a property of the sample, as $20$K
edges of the same graph give $2.06\times$ on cycle-4
(Section~\ref{sec:suite}). On our data the gains came from \emph{asymmetric degree structure in cyclic joins} (JOB-medium and TPC-H-like cases in the extended version). Synthetic power-law graphs improve more (up to
$614\times$ at cycle-8) than real data, where the median over the long
suites is $1.8$--$8.9\times$ (tails $157\times$ on Hetionet, $34\times$
on IMDb): every synthetic relation is drawn with one shared Zipf exponent ($\alpha{=}1$ in the headline sweep), so every middle factor of Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} exceeds $1$ at once, whereas real relations differ in skew.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Block decomposition methods}
CEG's pessimistic variant CEG$_M$~\cite{chen2022ceg,salihoglu2026ceg} models estimation as a shortest path over max-degree weights of base relations and small joins (its optimistic CEG$_O$, like Graphflow's catalog~\cite{mhedhbi2019graphflow}, uses average degrees of such joins); multi-way block extensions~\cite{hceg2027} generalize this to groups of relations via hyperpaths. Both use only $\ell_\infty$ statistics. LpH-CEG replaces these scalar weights with LP-optimized, interface-conditioned $\ell_p$-norm products and
gives a sufficient condition for the LP to improve: cyclic queries with
redundant coverage (Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic}).

\paragraph{Information-theoretic bounds}
The AGM bound~\cite{atserias2008agm} uses fractional edge covers
with relation cardinalities ($\ell_1$-norms).
PANDA~\cite{abokhamis2017panda} and MOLP~\cite{joglekar2016molp} add
max-degree constraints ($\ell_\infty$-norms).
LpBound~\cite{zhang2025lpbound} generalizes to all $\ell_p$-norms,
showing that intermediate norms capture degree skew that max-degree
misses.
Since then, Im et al.~\cite{im2025simple} compute the polymatroid bound for simple degree
constraints by a polynomial flow LP and show the non-simple case
($|X|{\le}2$, $|Y|{\le}3$, the shape of a $bs{=}2$ block constraint)
as hard as the general polymatroid bound, which is why our flow bound over blocks fixes an attribute order;
CorrBound~\cite{mayer2026corrbound} adds inner products of degree
vectors across join columns (a two-relation statistic in our
terms) to the entropic program and reports $6.7$--$16.3\times$ lower error than LpBound on cyclic subgraph-matching queries; there, block statistics are a median $3.0\times$ tighter than its published estimates, and on our suites they add $63\times$ over a proxy with exact pair-join sizes (three norms, flow LP) (Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).  The
$\ell_1$-norm of a $bs{=}2$ block sequence \emph{is} CorrBound's inner
product (the join size).  The shared-interface block norms
$\sum_b \deg_R(b)^p\deg_S(b)^p$ are $\ell_p$-weighted inner products and
may fall within CorrBound's vocabulary; the one-side and common-neighbour statistics need not, since they depend on \emph{which} values co-occur, not only how often (for the common-neighbour $\ell_2$ we give a separating example in the extended version)---and the latter needs the join that CorrBound's
sketches avoid (Section~\ref{sec:stats-vs-inference}).
Lin and Wang~\cite{lin2025ambidextrous} count claw \emph{pairs}, a richer statistic again.
SafeBound~\cite{deeds2023safebound} deploys degree-sequence
bounds~\cite{deeds2023dsb} with
predicates on relational workloads, following pessimistic
CE~\cite{cai2019pessimistic}; we target graph patterns and a per-step
decomposition.

Theorem~\ref{thm:composition} applies Abo Khamis et al.'s entropy framework~\cite{abokhamis2024lpnorm} to block joins.

\paragraph{Summaries and learned estimators}
COLOR~\cite{deeds2025color} summarizes and GNCE~\cite{schwabe2024gnce}, SPACE~\cite{aytimur2025space} and FactorJoin~\cite{wu2023factorjoin} learn point estimates without a guarantee, for which pessimistic bounds are the safety fallback~\cite{abokhamis2025pessimistic}; PathCE~\cite{wang2025pathce} keeps an upper bound over path summaries.

\paragraph{Join evaluation algorithms}
Yannakakis+~\cite{wang2025yannakakis} (acyclic queries, the complement of our setting) and CompactLTJ~\cite{arroyuelo2025compactltj} evaluate joins; Capelli et al.'s sampling variant~\cite{capelli2025simple} takes tree-superadditive prefix bounds, which our residuals could supply if they qualify, though they do not generally beat max-degree ordering in our $66$-query study.

\section{Practical Considerations}
\label{sec:practical}

\paragraph{When to use LpH-CEG}
Use it on cyclic queries with moderately skewed data, with $\mathcal{P} = \{1,2,\infty\}$ for the cover LP (the entropic and polymatroid programs gain from more norms): gains are rare on triangles and grow with cycle length; without a cycle Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} does not apply, though intermediate norms can still help (Section~\ref{sec:whycyclic}).

\paragraph{Under predicates} Block statistics of unfiltered relations stay valid under a selection (degrees only shrink) but loosen. With a production-year predicate on every movie variable of the $40$ IMDb $|V|{=}6$ cycles, exact filtered single-relation statistics plus a predicate-free block catalog (eleven norms) put the polymatroid bound a median $1.9\times$ below LpBound on $40/40$ queries when $46\%$ of rows pass ($\text{year}{\ge}2005$), but only $1.03\times$ ($22/40$) at $18\%$ ($\text{year}{<}1995$); per-predicate block statistics stay $4.2$--$5.3\times$ below, and no bound falls below the exact count ($240$ checks). Selective predicates thus need per-predicate block statistics, the main obstacle to $bs{\ge}2$ under predicates, not the program.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Replacing a max-degree product with a fractional cover LP is worth a
great deal ($614\times$ at synthetic cycle-8), but on single-relation
statistics little is left after it: the cover LP is optimal among
fixed-hyperpath weightings valid on every database without self-joins
(Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity}) yet sits $1.25$--$1.43\times$
(synthetic) and a median $2.7$--$6.8\times$ on Hetionet and IMDb
($59\times$ on AIDS) above the entropic optimum, which LpBound's flow LP
reaches in polynomial time. The headroom is in the statistics. Block
statistics put even the cover LP a median $46$--$920\times$ below LpBound
on Hetionet ($1.3$--$6.8\times$ on IMDb), and the polymatroid program over
them, valid for every $p$, reaches $236$--$10{,}200\times$ and
$9.7$--$87\times$, $15$--$24\times$ on G-CARE, and a median $3.0\times$
over CorrBound-e on CorrBound's own workload: richer statistics reopen
the program. A median $48\%$ and $79\%$ of the log-gain lies outside a pairwise inner-product proxy for
CorrBound's statistics, and with one sparse product per block the full catalog
costs about $8\times$ LpBound's collection. Our $\ell_p$ flow LP is a polynomial-time candidate (equal to the optimum on $633$ of $862$ queries), checked per query rather than proved valid; past $|V|{\approx}12$ the cover LP is the guarantee.

\emph{Limits.} Our evidence is binary graph patterns: Hetionet and IMDb
slices ($3$--$20$K rows, $200$--$1{,}000$ films), G-CARE's AIDS and YAGO
($174$ YAGO queries exceed our prototype; validity uses our counts) and
CorrBound's DBLP queries. Neither our LpBound reimplementation nor our block statistics support general predicates (Section~\ref{sec:practical}), so
STATS-CEB~\cite{han2021stats} and JOB's own queries are out of scope.
Collecting the full catalog grows somewhat faster than the data ($5.1\times$ the time for $4.4\times$ the rows). Bound-guided join orders lose to DuckDB on IMDb: this maps headroom; it is not an optimizer.
